## Why

The harness's todos seam proved a reader law that is right — an
unterminated last line reads as ABSENCE, a hole anywhere earlier is
REFUSED — and then the real-composition gate found the law is not enough
alone. The next `append` lands on the end of the partial line, so the tear
and the new record fuse into ONE undecodable line in the MIDDLE of the
document. A Todo that replayed fine after one boot refuses to replay at
the boot after, and its store fails to activate. Reproduced deterministically
in `jinn-todo`'s `an_append_onto_a_torn_tail_makes_a_hole_the_reader_refuses`
and driven through the real daemon in the composition suite.

The harness works around it by HEALING on adoption: a replay reporting
`torn_tail_bytes > 0` triggers a full `fs::write` of the whole prefix. No
record is lost — by the reader's law those bytes were never a record — and
`describe` reports `healed-tails` so nothing is discarded in silence. But
it is a REWRITE of an append-only document costing the whole document's
bytes per heal, which is the wrong shape on a long-lived ledger.

## Scope

A `truncate(path, len, idempotency-key)` operation on `jinn:fs` that drops
exactly the suffix past `len`, so a store repairs a tear at the cost of the
tear rather than the document.

- **It never extends.** `len` greater than the current length REFUSES,
  typed, naming both lengths — it does not zero-fill and does not succeed
  silently. `len` equal to the current length is a no-op that is still
  ledgered, because "nothing needed doing" and "it did not run" must be
  distinguishable.
- **It is an effect** under R5 with the same scope check as `write`,
  ledgered with the path, both lengths, and the idempotency key.
- **Replay is by key.** A repeated `truncate` with the same key on an
  already-truncated document succeeds without re-truncating; the same key
  against a DIFFERENT length REFUSES rather than picking one.
- **Authority must be PROVEN not widened.** The argument that truncate is
  strictly weaker than the `write` a plugin already holds is plausible and
  is exactly the kind of claim this program has been burned by (the M2-K8
  bind-range hull, pre-blessed by the COO and then proved wrong by the
  verifier). So it is acceptance, not a preamble: demonstrate that a
  `truncate` grant admits no path, and no outcome, that the holder's
  existing `write` grant does not already admit. If the demonstration
  fails, say so and the operation gets its own grant.

## Acceptance

- `truncate` declared in `wit/plugin.wit` and `contracts/jinn-fs`,
  additive and versioned per R12.
- A fixture through the REAL daemon: a document with a torn tail is
  truncated to the last complete record and the next append replays clean
  — the harness's fuse-into-a-hole case, made unreachable.
- Red-first each refusal: extending REFUSES naming both lengths; a
  same-key replay at a different length REFUSES; an out-of-scope path
  REFUSES and is distinguishable from both.
- The equal-length no-op appears in the ledger.
- The authority demonstration above, with its result stated either way.
- Ratchet no regressions; gates + loom + miri + `--locked`; two-key clean;
  ceilings per this card; contract deltas additive per R12.

## Out of scope

A conditional `append` that refuses unless the document ends on a given
byte. It is the more elegant shape — it makes the hazard unreachable by
construction rather than repairable after the fact — but it is a second
new operation, and R10 says small and boring. If the truncate work shows
the repair path is still awkward, that becomes its own finding and its own
card, on evidence. Also out: compaction, rotation, any change to the
reader law, and any harness-side change.
