## Why now

K9 closed the *addressable-while-restarting* window. The harness then hit
the next one, and this time with a transcript: the settings owner
re-declares on every alarm wake, which is a call **into** the provider that
is at that moment dispatching a `changed` notice **into** the owner. Each
parks on the other and both die on the guest deadline. `PATCH
/v1/settings/cron` now answers (K9's work), but the deeper cycle remains and
the harness carries a named `#[ignore]` because of it.

Two facts the harness established that this card must respect:
- **`Emit` is not an escape.** The kernel awaits every listener delivery
  end-to-end in every mode; fire-and-forget discards the *answer*, never the
  *wait*. Any fix that assumes an emit returns early is wrong.
- **Whether the loser recovers is incidental.** One transcript showed the
  deadlocked fiber never recovering; a second showed it recovering, but only
  because that patch happened to target the fiber's own entry so the loader
  already owed it a restart. A fix must not rely on the loader happening to
  owe a restart.

## Scope

- **Detect the cycle, refuse the edge.** A reply-expecting dispatch that
  would close a cycle — the target is, transitively, already awaiting the
  caller — REFUSES immediately with a typed reason naming both ends, rather
  than parking until the guest deadline. Refusal is ledgered like any other.
- **The refusal carries the caller's next move**, per the K9 vocabulary: a
  cycle is not a restart and not a scope error; it is its own reason and the
  caller must not blindly retry.
- **Observable, not inferred:** `jinn:introspect` exposes enough of the
  wait graph that an operator can see why a call refused. Additive per R12.
- Red-first: reproduce the harness's exact shape — owner re-declares into a
  provider that is mid-notify into the owner — and assert a typed refusal
  inside the deadline, on both orderings.

- Fixture through the real daemon reproducing the harness two-way shape:

  from `Restarting` and from a scope refusal, on both orderings; introspect

- The harness's `#[ignore]`d settings recovery test passes at the bumped pin
  — verified by actually running it, not by assuming.
- Ratchet: no regressions; gates + loom + miri (non-FFI) + `--locked`;
  two-key clean; ceilings per this card; 300-line cap (hard for src, soft for

- Contract deltas additive and versioned (R12).

## Out of scope

4-remainder/6/7/9/13/29/30; any harness-side change.


## COO RULING — round 1, 2026-08-30 (amended in-file the moment the verdict landed)

The implementer escalated with a per-surface breakdown rather than golfing, and
the verifier independently measured the same **net 783** and raised it as a
Blocker. Both were right to. Two rulings:

**1. The meter was misstated, and R10 governs.** R10's metric note (2026-08-25)
excludes "the conformance-harness lane, loom-only models, and cfg(test) code"
from the kernel-core count. This card restated the exclusion more narrowly as
files under `tests/` or named `tests.rs`, which would count
`jinnd-wasm/src/waits/cycle_model.rs` (76 lines, cfg(test) + loom-only). **A card
cannot narrow a law.** The exclusion applies: measured production net is
**707**, not 783. This is a correction of the card's arithmetic, not a
concession.

**2. The ceiling is re-priced 700 → 760 on evidence.** 707 still exceeded 700,
and asking for a 7-line trim is exactly the golfing R10 forbids ("never to
incentivize golfing containment or honesty code"). The evidence for a re-price
is independent, not the implementer's say-so: the verifier confirmed no
unrelated surface, no personal data, no file over the 300-line cap, and that the
diff maps to the fixture/refusal/ledger/introspect/versioning acceptance, with
R1/R3/R5/R9/R10/R11 all clean. The breakdown shows this card mandated a wait
graph, a loom model, typed refusal on the call lane, all-or-nothing refusal on
the dispatch walk, one graph on both surfaces, a WIT case + record, a contract op
with a version bump, and a fiber→entry naming seam — in one packet. Budgets are
priced before the work exists; this is the M1-P2 / M2-K9 precedent in the
decision log. **760 is the ceiling. Measured 707 passes.**

**3. The uncovered probe, flagged honestly rather than papered over.** The
implementer reported no dedicated test for *a cycle that forms concurrently with
a restart*, instead of claiming coverage it did not have. That interaction is the
intersection of K9's restart window and this card's cycle refusal, it is squarely
inside the stated THREAT MODEL (accidental conditions), and tests cost nothing
against the meter. **Add it.** If it cannot be made deterministic, do NOT ship a
flaky probe and do NOT retry it until green — name it a known limit in the
README with the reason, per the PLA-320 discipline. An honest named limit beats a
test that passes by luck.
