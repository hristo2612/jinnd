# M2-K21 — the kernel's inbound surface authenticates nobody: any process that can reach the socket is the operator

**Milestone:** M2/M3-entry — the SECOND of §7's two named M3 blockers ·
**Owner:** kernel-dev · **Status:** ready · **Binding rules:** R1, R3, R5,
R9, R10, R11, R12, Laws 1/2/3 ·
**Design decisions in scope: ONE** — what proves a caller may issue this

unauthorized facade item is an automatic blocker · **Size:** declare a budget

`cargo build --workspace --locked`.

## The gap

`jinnd` has no authentication and no authorization anywhere. Anything that can
reach the daemon's inbound surface can issue any dispatch, with the full
authority of the operator. §7 names this as one of the two structural blockers
against M3's acceptance — *"an instance runs a full day on jinnd alone"* — and
M2-K14/K15 have now closed the other one.

## SCOPE — decided by the COO, do not widen it

**In scope: authenticate the CALLER of the kernel's inbound surface, deny by
default, and ledger every decision.**

**Explicitly OUT of scope, and not deferred by oversight:**
- User accounts, roles, RBAC, groups, tenancy. There is one operator.
- Remote authentication. There is no remote surface; K15 gives the kernel
  outbound TLS, not an inbound listener.
- Delegation, impersonation, or capability attenuation between plugins. That
  is a later milestone and a different design.

R10 governs: this must be the smallest thing that closes the blocker. If the
design starts to resemble an identity system, it has left the card.

## THREAT MODEL — state the limit, do not paper over it

**In model:** a process on this machine that is not the harness reaching the
socket, whether by accident, misconfiguration, or a mistaken second instance;
and a future remote surface being added without the author noticing there was
never a check.

**Explicitly NOT in model: a malicious process running as the same uid.** It
can read whatever the harness can read, including any token on disk. **Say so
in the contract and in the code comment.** A guarantee that cannot hold against
same-uid is not a guarantee, and claiming it would be exactly the vacuity this
program has spent six instances learning to refuse.

## What must hold

- **Deny by default.** An unauthenticated dispatch is refused, typed, before it
  reaches any effect. Prove the refusal happens *before* the effect, not after.
- **A typed refusal distinct from every other refusal class** — not folded into
  `denied` (allowlist) or `failed` (transport). Assert its own precondition.
- **Law 2: every decision is ledgered** — grant and refusal alike, with the
  caller identity in whatever form the design settles on. **No credential,
  token, or secret material in the ledger row** — name and digest only, the
  M2-K14 redaction precedent.
- **No bypass.** No env var, profile field, build flag or test seam that
  disables the check in a release build. Prove the seam is absent from a
  release build with a test, not by inspection — the M2-K15 precedent.
- **A rotated or revoked credential stops working** without restarting the
  daemon, or the contract says plainly that it does not.

## Evidence standard — inherited, not restated

Checkable red-first (failing test in its own commit, or verbatim output).
Mutation-verified coverage: **for each mutation, the file:line changed, the
exact command, and the verbatim failing output, posted as a Todo comment.**
Contract text asserted by PARSING, never `contains` — six instances have
shipped in this program, one of them written against a warning printed in its
own card. Identical signature is not identical observation.
