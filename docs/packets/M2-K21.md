## The gap

`jinnd` has no authentication and no authorization anywhere. Anything that can
reach the daemon's inbound surface can issue any dispatch, with the full
authority of the operator. §7 names this as one of the two structural blockers
against M3's acceptance — *"an instance runs a full day on jinnd alone"* — and
M2-K14/K15 have now closed the other one.

## SCOPE — decided by the COO, do not widen it

**In scope: authenticate the CALLER of the kernel's inbound surface, deny by
default, and ledger every decision.**

**Explicitly OUT of scope, and not deferred by oversight:**
- User accounts, roles, RBAC, groups, tenancy. There is one operator.
- Remote authentication. There is no remote surface; K15 gives the kernel
  outbound TLS, not an inbound listener.
- Delegation, impersonation, or capability attenuation between plugins. That
  is a later milestone and a different design.

R10 governs: this must be the smallest thing that closes the blocker. If the
design starts to resemble an identity system, it has left the card.

## THREAT MODEL — state the limit, do not paper over it

**In model:** a process on this machine that is not the harness reaching the
socket, whether by accident, misconfiguration, or a mistaken second instance;
and a future remote surface being added without the author noticing there was
never a check.

**Explicitly NOT in model: a malicious process running as the same uid.** It
can read whatever the harness can read, including any token on disk. **Say so
in the contract and in the code comment.** A guarantee that cannot hold against
same-uid is not a guarantee, and claiming it would be exactly the vacuity this
program has spent six instances learning to refuse.

## What must hold

- **Deny by default, at the ONE decision point the kernel owns.** The kernel
  holds no inbound listener — a transport plugin does, through `jinn:net`, and
  the kernel sees only bytes — so the kernel supplies the AUTHORITY:
  `jinn:auth.verify` refuses unless proven, records nothing but its own
  decision row, and a plugin never granted `jinn:auth` cannot obtain a
  principal at all. The contract text states the transport's obligation in
  plain words: no dispatch on a connection's behalf before `verify` grants.
  *"Refused before any effect reaches the inbound surface"* is proven where
  the door is — in the transport plugin, by the composition suite — and that
  proof is **harness packet 2.8 (PLA-343)**, not this one. (COO ruling
  2026-09-02; the original wording asked this packet to prove a property
  the card's own scope exclusion — no delegation between plugins — makes
  unprovable inside the kernel.)
- **A typed refusal distinct from every other refusal class** — not folded into
  `denied` (allowlist) or `failed` (transport). Assert its own precondition.
- **Law 2: every decision is ledgered** — grant and refusal alike, with the
  caller identity in whatever form the design settles on. **No credential,
  token, or secret material in the ledger row** — name and digest only, the
  M2-K14 redaction precedent.
- **No bypass.** No env var, profile field, build flag or test seam that
  disables the check in a release build. Prove the seam is absent from a
  release build with a test, not by inspection — the M2-K15 precedent.
- **A rotated or revoked credential stops working** without restarting the
  daemon, or the contract says plainly that it does not.

## COO amendment — 2026-09-02, after round 1 (binding; supersedes conflicting text above)

Round 1 came back `rework` with three Blockers. Two were defects in this card,
one is a scope split; all three are ruled here so round 2 is not spent on them.

1. **Facade — AUTHORIZED.** `LedgerEventKind::AuthDecided { name, presented
   (sha256 hex), granted }`, additive, exactly as built at `d8f273a`. The card
   mandated "every decision ledgered, name and digest only" and authorized no
   means — the fourth instance of that shape in this program, all mine.
2. **Budget — ACCEPTED at ≤ 215 added** on the implementer's declared meter.
   Round 2 adds no production lines by design; if it must, re-declare before
   the edit with the same meter.
3. **"Advisory, not a gate" — the finding is correct about the property and
   wrong about the owner.** The kernel cannot gate a transport plugin's own
   granted calls without per-call principal propagation, which is delegation
   between plugins and OUT of this card's scope by my own ruling. So the
   kernel's deliverable is the authority (one decision point, deny by default,
   ledgered, no bypass, no principal without the grant) and the door's proof
   is the harness's: **PLA-343 / harness 2.8** — `jinn-api-http` calls
   `jinn:auth.verify` before issuing any dispatch on a connection's behalf,
   proven by the composition suite with a wrong credential producing a typed
   refusal, zero effects, and one `AuthDecided` row, and by a mutation that
   removes the call and fails the suite. M3's second blocker closes when BOTH
   land, and §7's audit reads them together.

**Round 2 scope, exhaustively:** (a) the guest test at
`crates/jinnd-daemon/tests/auth.rs` keeps its facts but must SAY what they
prove — a wrong credential yields no principal, the plugin's own grants are
untouched *by design*, the door is the transport's — in the test name and its
doc, so a reader cannot mistake the demonstration for an oversight;
(b) the contract's "WHAT A TRANSPORT OWES" paragraph names PLA-343 as the
proof's home; (c) the R10 Minor: split `auth_cap/tests.rs` at the
source-scan seam; (d) nothing else. Behaviour-free throughout; `pass` = 0
Blockers applies.
